# Site Rules (All Machines) – Operator & Maintenance Manual  
*Section: site_rules*  

---  

## Overview  

This section defines the mandatory site‑wide rules that apply to the operation, movement, and support of **all** heavy‑equipment types. Compliance with these rules protects personnel, equipment, and the environment, and ensures that any incident or near‑miss is documented and investigated promptly.  

---  

### PROCEDURE SR01: RADIO COMMUNICATION PROTOCOL  

1. **Radio Allocation**  
   - Assign a dedicated two‑way radio to each machine and to each spotter or supervisor involved in the operation.  
   - Use a single, site‑wide channel for all equipment‑related traffic; reserve separate channels for emergency services.  

2. **Call Sign Format**  
   - Begin every transmission with the machine’s **call sign** (e.g., “MACHINE‑01”) followed by the speaker’s role (e.g., “OPERATOR”).  
   - End each transmission with “OVER” to indicate that a reply is expected, or “OUT” when the conversation is complete.  

3. **Standard Phraseology**  
   | Phrase | Intended Meaning |
   |--------|-----------------|
   | “CLEAR TO MOVE” | Permission granted to start travel or reposition. |
   | “STOP MOVEMENT” | Immediate halt of all machine motion. |
   | “OBSTRUCTION AHEAD” | Hazard detected in the machine’s path. |
   | “REQUEST SPOTTER” | Operator needs a spotter for the next maneuver. |
   | “INCIDENT REPORT” | Initiate the incident‑reporting process (see Procedure SR04). |

4. **Transmission Discipline**  
   - Speak clearly, use a moderate pace, and keep messages concise (no more than two sentences).  
   - Do **not** transmit while operating the machine’s controls; pause the operation or use a hands‑free device.  

> **WARNING:**  
> Transmitting while operating controls can cause loss of situational awareness and increase the risk of collision. Always pause machine motion before speaking, unless an emergency requires immediate verbal warning.  

5. **Radio Failure**  
   - If a radio fails, switch to the pre‑designated backup channel within 5 seconds and announce the change.  
   - Report the malfunction to the site supervisor at the earliest safe opportunity.  

---  

### PROCEDURE SR02: SPOTTER USAGE  

1. **Spotter Assignment**  
   - Assign at least one qualified spotter for any operation where the machine’s view is obstructed, the load is elevated, or the work area is confined.  
   - Spotters must be equipped with a functional two‑way radio and wear high‑visibility PPE (see Procedure SR03).  

2. **Spotter Positioning**  
   - Stand **outside** the machine’s swing radius and maintain an unobstructed line of sight to the load or work area.  
   - Keep a safe distance from the machine’s moving parts; if a closer position is required for visibility, the spotter must wear additional protective gloves and a hard hat.  

3. **Spotter Signals**  
   - Use the radio to convey the same standard phraseology as operators (see Procedure SR01).  
   - When radio communication is unavailable, employ hand signals that have been pre‑approved by the site safety plan (e.g., “STOP” – arm raised, “PROCEED” – arm extended forward).  

> **WARNING:**  
> Spotters must never place themselves in the path of moving loads. A mis‑positioned spotter can become a struck‑by hazard.  

4. **Spotter Rotation**  
   - Rotate spotters every **30 minutes** to prevent fatigue, unless the task duration is less than 30 minutes.  
   - Document each rotation in the daily shift log.  

5. **Spotter Withdrawal**  
   - If a spotter feels unsafe, they must immediately announce “STOP MOVEMENT” and retreat to a safe location. The operator must halt the machine until the issue is resolved.

### PROCEDURE SR03: PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT (PPE) REQUIREMENTS  

| PPE Item | Minimum Requirement | When Required |
|----------|--------------------|---------------|
| Hard hat | ANSI A‑116.1 (or equivalent) | All personnel on site |
| Safety glasses / goggles | Impact‑resistant lenses | When operating or observing machines |
| Hearing protection | Earplugs or earmuffs with ≥ 85 dB attenuation | During operation of noisy equipment |
| High‑visibility clothing | Class 2 (fluorescent orange or yellow) | All personnel within the designated safety zone around moving equipment |
| Safety footwear | Steel‑toe, slip‑resistant | All personnel on the work surface |
| Gloves | Cut‑resistant, oil‑resistant | When handling loads, operating controls, or assisting spotters |
| Fall protection | Full‑body harness with lanyard | When working at heights where fall risk is present or on elevated platforms |

1. **PPE Inspection**  
   - Inspect each PPE item before the start of the shift for damage, wear, or contamination.  
   - Replace any compromised item immediately; do not begin work with defective PPE.  

2. **PPE Donning Sequence**  
   - Put on safety footwear first, followed by gloves, hard hat, hearing protection, safety glasses, high‑visibility clothing, and finally any fall‑protection harness.  

3. **PPE Removal**  
   - Remove gloves and hearing protection before exiting the equipment area to avoid contaminating other zones.  
   - Store hard hats and glasses in designated racks to prevent damage.  

> **WARNING:**  
> Failure to wear the required PPE is a direct violation of site safety policy and may result in disciplinary action, as well as increased risk of injury.  

4. **PPE Documentation**  
   - Record any PPE deficiencies or replacements in the daily safety log.

### PROCEDURE SR04: INCIDENT AND NEAR‑MISS REPORTING  

1. **Immediate Action**  
   - Stop all work in the area of the incident or near‑miss.  
   - Secure the machine and any loads to prevent further movement.  

2. **Notification**  
   - Use the radio to announce “INCIDENT REPORT” to the site supervisor and safety officer.  
   - If radio communication is unavailable, physically locate the nearest supervisor and report verbally.  

3. **Initial Documentation (within 15 minutes)**  
   - Complete the **Incident/Near‑Miss Form** with the following fields:  
     - Date & time  
     - Machine ID and operator name  
     - Spotter name (if applicable)  
     - Description of event (objective facts only)  
     - Immediate actions taken  
     - Witnesses (names and contact)  

4. **Investigation**  
   - The safety officer leads a root‑cause analysis within **24 hours** of the report.  
   - Collect photographs, radio logs, and any relevant sensor data from the machine.  

5. **Corrective Actions**  
   - Develop a corrective‑action plan that includes:  
     - Engineering controls (e.g., guard installation)  
     - Administrative controls (e.g., revised procedure)  
     - Training updates (e.g., refresher on radio protocol)  
   - Assign responsibility and a completion date; track progress in the corrective‑action register.  

6. **Follow‑Up**  
   - Conduct a debrief with all involved personnel within **48 hours**.  
   - Update the site‑wide safety bulletin with lessons learned, ensuring anonymity of individuals where appropriate.  

> **WARNING:**  
> Under‑reporting or delayed reporting of incidents and near‑misses undermines safety culture and may lead to repeat events. All personnel are required to report promptly, regardless of perceived severity.  

7. **Record Retention**  
   - Keep all incident and near‑miss records for a minimum of **5 years** in the site safety archive.

## Summary  

Adhering to the radio communication protocol, employing qualified spotters, wearing the prescribed PPE, and promptly reporting any incident or near‑miss are the foundational site rules that safeguard all heavy‑equipment operations. Consistent application of these procedures reduces risk, improves response times, and fosters a culture of safety across the entire worksite.
