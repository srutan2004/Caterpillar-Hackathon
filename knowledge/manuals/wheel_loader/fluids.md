### PROCEDURE FL‑001: Verify Fuel Level Before Operation  

1. **Locate the fuel gauge** on the instrument panel.  
2. Observe the needle or digital read‑out.  
3. If the gauge indicates a low level of the tank capacity, plan a refuel stop before starting heavy work.  
4. Record the current fuel level in the daily log sheet.  

> **WARNING:** Operating the loader with a fuel level below the minimum recommended may cause fuel starvation, engine shutdown, and possible damage to the fuel pump.

### PROCEDURE FL‑002: Refuel the Loader  

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Park the loader on a level, well‑ventilated surface away from open flames or sparks. |
| 2 | Engage the parking brake and turn the engine off. |
| 3 | Remove the fuel‑cap plug using the supplied wrench. Inspect the cap gasket for cracks or damage; replace if necessary. |
| 4 | Position a **clean, approved fuel dispensing hose** to the filler neck. |
| 5 | Open the dispenser valve and begin fueling. The **maximum flow rate** is **60 L /min**; do not exceed this rate. |
| 6 | Continuously monitor the fuel gauge or a calibrated dip‑stick to avoid over‑filling. |
| 7 | When the tank reaches full (290 L) or the dispenser automatically stops, close the dispenser valve. |
| 8 | Replace the fuel‑cap securely and tighten to the torque specification (see the Fastener Table in the Mechanical section). |
| 9 | Clean any spilled fuel with an approved absorbent material before leaving the area. |
|10| Log the amount of fuel added, time of refuel, and the name of the operator in the fuel‑log book. |

> **WARNING:**  
> • Never smoke, use a spark‑producing tool, or operate a mobile phone near the refuel area.  
> • Fuel is highly flammable; any spill must be contained and cleaned immediately.  
> • Over‑filling can cause fuel to expand and leak, creating a fire hazard.

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Park the loader on a flat, dry surface and engage the parking brake. |
| 2 | Turn off the machine and disconnect the main power switch. |
| 3 | Connect the charger cable to the loader’s charging inlet, ensuring the connector is clean and free of debris. |

### PROCEDURE FL‑007: Routine Fluid Inspection  

1. **Engine oil** – Check level with dip‑stick when the engine is warm and off for a short period. Top up to the “Full” mark using the specified oil.  
2. **Hydraulic oil** – Inspect the sight‑glass on the hydraulic reservoir; the oil should be clear and within the “Min–Max” marks.  
3. **Transmission oil** – Verify level through the fill plug; do not exceed the “Max” line.  
4. **Coolant** – Observe the coolant reservoir; the level must be between the “Low” and “High” marks.  

If any fluid is low, add the correct type as listed in **Procedure FL‑006**. Record the addition in the **Daily Maintenance Log**.

> **WARNING:** Never add a fluid that does not meet the specification; doing so may void the warranty and cause equipment damage.

### PROCEDURE FL‑008: Drain and Refill Fuel Tank (Major Service)  

1. Position the loader on a containment pad to capture any spilled fuel.  
2. Connect a certified fuel‑drain pump to the tank’s drain valve.  
3. Open the valve and pump the fuel into an **approved fuel storage container**.  
4. Close the drain valve, purge the system with dry nitrogen for a short period to remove residual fuel vapour.  
5. Re‑fill the tank following **Procedure FL‑002**.  

> **WARNING:**  
> • Perform this operation only in a well‑ventilated area with fire‑extinguishing equipment nearby.  
> • Ensure all personnel wear flame‑resistant clothing and appropriate respiratory protection.

### PROCEDURE FL‑010: Emergency Spill Response  

1. **Stop** all fueling or charging operations immediately.  
2. **Evacuate** personnel from the immediate area.  
3. Deploy an **approved absorbent pad** over the spill; cover the entire affected surface.  
4. Once the spill is contained, place the saturated absorbent in a **hazardous‑waste container** and label according to local regulations.  
5. Notify the site safety officer and complete an **Incident Report** within the required timeframe.  

> **WARNING:** Failure to contain a fuel spill can lead to fire, environmental contamination, and regulatory penalties.
