## Transport and Lifting (Transport)

This section describes the safe handling of the wheel loader when moving it on a trailer, securing it for road transport, and using the factory‑approved lifting points. All operations must be performed by trained personnel using equipment rated for the loader’s **Operating Mass** of **18 200 kg**.

---

### PROCEDURE TL‑01: Loading the Loader onto a Trailer

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | **Position the trailer** on a level, firm surface. Verify that the trailer’s rated payload exceeds the loader’s operating mass (≥ 18 200 kg). |
| 2 | Align the trailer’s rear deck with the loader’s rear axle. Use wheel chocks on the trailer deck to prevent movement while the loader is positioned. |
| 3 | Deploy a **hydraulic or manual winch** with a rated line pull of at least **30 000 N** (≈ 3 × operating mass) to pull the loader onto the deck. Attach the winch hook to the loader’s **front lifting point** (see Procedure TL‑03). |
| 4 | Slowly pull the loader forward, keeping it centered on the trailer deck. Stop when the front axle is approximately **300 mm** from the trailer’s front edge. |
| 5 | Drive the loader forward under its own power (if engine is running) or use a low‑speed push‑behind vehicle to move the rear axle onto the deck, ensuring the rear wheels are fully on the trailer. |
| 6 | Once both axles are on the deck, engage the loader’s parking brake and shut off the engine. Verify that the loader is level (within ± 2°) using a bubble level placed on the cab floor. |
| 7 | Proceed to **Procedure TL‑02** to secure the loader for transport. |

> **WARNING:** Never attempt to load a wheel loader onto a trailer that is not rated for at least **18 200 kg**. Over‑loading can cause structural failure of the trailer frame and result in loss of control during transport.

---

### PROCEDURE TL‑02: Securing (Tie‑Down) the Loader for Road Transport

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Identify the **four approved tie‑down points**: two on the front lifting lugs and two on the rear lifting lugs. These are reinforced steel plates bolted to the frame and are the only locations approved for load restraint. |
| 2 | Select **high‑strength ratchet straps** with a working load limit (WLL) of **≥ 30 000 N** (≥ 1.6 × operating mass). Do not use chains, ropes, or improvised devices. |
| 3 | Attach one strap to each front lifting point and run it forward over the trailer’s front cross‑member, then back to the same lifting point, forming a “U‑shaped” loop. Tighten the strap until the loader cannot be moved forward or backward by hand. |
| 4 | Repeat the same procedure for the rear lifting points, routing the straps over the trailer’s rear cross‑member. |
| 5 | Install **two lateral (side‑to‑side) straps**: run each strap across the width of the loader, anchoring to the side rails of the trailer. Tension each strap until there is **no lateral movement** of the loader when a 100 N push is applied from either side. |
| 6 | Perform a **pre‑departure check**: pull each strap to confirm it holds the required tension, and verify that no strap is twisted or rubbing against the loader’s cab, hydraulic hoses, or under‑carriage components. |
| 7 | Document the strap WLL, serial numbers, and the date of the tie‑down in the transport log. |

> **WARNING:** Improperly tensioned straps can loosen during transit, causing the loader to shift and potentially damage the trailer, the loader, or other road users. Always use straps with a WLL that meets or exceeds the required value.

### PROCEDURE TL‑03: Using the Lifting Points for Hoisting

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Locate the **front and rear lifting points** on the loader’s frame. They are marked with a **“LIFT”** stencil and are positioned symmetrically about the centre of gravity. |
| 2 | Verify that the **lifting device** (crane, hoist, or forklift) has a rated capacity of at least **30 000 kg** (≈ 1.65 × operating mass). |
| 3 | Attach a **grade‑approved lifting sling** or **lifting beam** to each lifting point. The sling’s WLL must be ≥ 30 000 N. Do not use hooks or eye‑bolts that are not part of the factory‑installed lifting hardware. |
| 4 | Before lifting, perform a **visual inspection** of the lifting points for cracks, deformation, or corrosion. If any defect is found, abort the lift and report the condition. |
| 5 | Position the crane’s hook directly over the centre of the loader’s mass (mid‑point between front and rear lifting points). Use a **load‑moment indicator** to ensure the load is balanced; the indicator should read within ± 5 % of the crane’s rated capacity. |
| 6 | Slowly raise the loader a few centimetres off the ground and pause. Observe the loader for any unexpected movement or tilt. |
| 7 | Continue lifting to the required height, keeping the load level at all times. Do not swing the loader or allow it to rotate about the vertical axis. |
| 8 | When the loader is positioned on the trailer or in the storage area, lower it gently until the wheels make full contact with the support surface. Release the lifting device only after the loader is fully settled and the straps (if any) are engaged. |
| 9 | Remove the slings or lifting beam, and store them in a clean, dry location for future use. |

> **WARNING:** Lifting the loader from any point other than the factory‑approved lifting points can cause structural failure of the frame, leading to catastrophic loss of the machine and serious injury.

### PROCEDURE TL‑04: Unloading the Loader from a Trailer

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Park the trailer on a level surface and engage its parking brake. |
| 2 | Remove all tie‑down straps following the reverse order of **Procedure TL‑02**. Verify that each strap is fully released before moving it away from the loader. |
| 3 | Position a **low‑profile hydraulic jack** or a **tractor‑mounted winch** at the rear of the trailer, aligned with the loader’s rear lifting point. |
| 4 | Attach the winch hook to the rear lifting point (or use a lifting sling if a winch is not available). |
| 5 | Slowly pull the loader forward off the trailer deck, keeping it centered. |
| 6 | Once the front wheels clear the deck, drive the loader forward under its own power to a safe distance. |
| 7 | Perform a post‑unload inspection of the trailer deck, tie‑down points, and the loader’s lifting points for any damage. |

> **WARNING:** Do not allow the loader to roll uncontrolled off the trailer. Always keep the parking brake engaged until the loader is fully on the ground and the engine is started.
