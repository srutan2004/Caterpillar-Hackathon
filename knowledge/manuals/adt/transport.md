## Transport and Lifting (Transport)

This section describes the safe methods for **loading the articulated dump truck onto a trailer**, **securing it for road transport**, and **using the factory‑approved lifting points**. All personnel must be familiar with the procedures and the associated safety warnings before performing any transport operation.

---  

### PROCEDURE T01: Load the Truck onto a Trailer  

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | **Position the trailer** – Align the trailer’s deck with the loading ramp so that the ramp angle does not exceed the manufacturer‑approved limit. Verify that the trailer’s rated payload is ≥ **23 500 kg** (the truck’s operating mass). |
| 2 | **Inspect the ramp** – Check for cracks, corrosion, or missing fasteners. Ensure the ramp surface is clean and free of oil, water, or debris that could cause slippage. |
| 3 | **Engage the truck’s parking brake** and place the transmission in neutral. Verify that the steering lock is engaged. |
| 4 | **Drive the truck onto the ramp** at a controlled speed. Use a spotter to guide the driver and maintain a clear line of sight. |
| 5 | **Advance the truck onto the trailer deck** until the front axle is positioned over the trailer’s designated axle support brackets. The rear axle should be centered over the trailer’s rear support points. |
| 6 | **Lower the ramp** slowly, confirming that it seats fully on the trailer frame and that no gaps remain between ramp and deck. |
| 7 | **Perform a visual check** – Confirm that the truck is level and that the wheels are not overhanging the deck edges. |

> **WARNING:**  
> Do not attempt to load the truck if the trailer’s payload rating is less than **23 500 kg**. Overloading can cause structural failure of the trailer frame, leading to loss of control and severe injury.

### PROCEDURE T02: Secure the Truck (Tie‑Down)  

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | **Identify the approved tie‑down points** on the truck chassis. These are typically reinforced eyelets or D‑rings located on the frame rails near the front and rear axles. |
| 2 | **Select the proper tie‑down hardware** – Use high‑strength webbing or chain with a minimum breaking strength of **4 × operating mass** (≥ 94 000 N). The hardware must be rated for the load and compliant with local transport regulations. |
| 3 | **Attach the front tie‑downs** – Run two (or more) webbing straps from the trailer’s front anchor points to the truck’s front chassis tie‑down points. Tighten each strap using a calibrated tensioning device until the measured tension reaches the appropriate proportion of the strap’s rated breaking strength. |
| 4 | **Attach the rear tie‑downs** – Repeat the process using the trailer’s rear anchor points and the truck’s rear chassis tie‑down points. |
| 5 | **Cross‑tie the sides** – For additional stability, run a cross‑tie from the left side of the trailer to the right side of the truck’s frame rail, and vice‑versa. This prevents lateral movement during cornering. |
| 6 | **Check clearance** – Ensure that the tightened straps do not interfere with the steering linkage, brake lines, or any moving components. |
| 7 | **Perform a “pull test”** – Apply a gentle forward and rearward force on the truck to verify that no movement occurs. |
| 8 | **Document the tie‑down configuration** – Record the strap IDs, tension values, and inspection date on the transport log sheet. |

> **WARNING:**  
> Do not use damaged or corroded straps, chains, or shackles. A failed tie‑down can allow the truck to shift, causing trailer overturn or collision.  

> **WARNING:**  
> Never exceed the trailer’s maximum allowable tongue weight. Over‑loading the tongue can lead to trailer sway and loss of control.

### PROCEDURE T03: Lifting the Truck Using Factory‑Approved Points  

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | **Locate the lifting points** – The truck is equipped with two reinforced lifting lugs on the chassis frame, one forward of the front axle and one aft of the rear axle. They are marked with a “LIFT” stencil and are designed for use with a single‑point or spread‑beam crane. |
| 2 | **Select the crane** – Use a crane with a rated lifting capacity of at least **1.5 × operating mass** to provide a safety margin. Verify that the crane’s hook and sling are rated for the same capacity. |
| 3 | **Inspect lifting accessories** – Check slings, hooks, and shackles for wear, cracks, or deformation. Replace any compromised components before use. |
| 4 | **Attach the lifting slings** – Position a heavy‑duty synthetic or wire rope sling around each lifting lug. The sling should be centered on the lug and secured with a proper hitch (e.g., a bowline knot for synthetic rope). |
| 5 | **Connect the slings to the crane** – Use a spread‑beam or dual‑hook arrangement to keep the load balanced. Ensure that the angle between each sling and the vertical stays within the manufacturer‑approved limit to avoid excessive sling tension. |
| 6 | **Perform a pre‑lift test** – Apply a small load to confirm that the slings are seated correctly and that the truck remains stable. |
| 7 | **Lift the truck** – Raise the load slowly, keeping the crane’s boom within its rated load chart for the current radius. Stop the lift when the truck is clear of the ground and any obstacles. |
| 8 | **Swing and position** – If the truck must be moved laterally, use a controlled swing motion. Do not exceed a swing radius that would place the load beyond the crane’s rated capacity. |
| 9 | **Lower the truck** – When placing the truck onto a trailer, lower it until the chassis contacts the deck evenly. Release the slings only after the truck is fully supported by the trailer. |
| 10 | **Document the lift** – Record crane model, capacity, sling type, and any observations (e.g., unusual noises, sling wear) on the lift report. |

> **WARNING:**  
> Lifting the truck from any point other than the factory‑approved lifting lugs can cause frame deformation, loss of structural integrity, and catastrophic failure of the lifting equipment.  

> **WARNING:**  
> Never exceed the crane’s rated capacity. Over‑loading can cause boom collapse, hook failure, or tip‑over of the crane.

### General Safety Notes  

1. **Personnel protection** – All crew members must wear high‑visibility clothing, safety boots, hard hats, and hearing protection when operating cranes or loading trailers.  
2. **Communication** – Use a standardized hand‑signal system or two‑way radios. A designated “spotter” must maintain visual contact with the driver and crane operator at all times.  
3. **Environmental conditions** – Do not perform loading, tie‑down, or lifting operations in high winds, heavy rain, or on uneven ground.  
4. **Inspection before each use** – Conduct a pre‑operation inspection of the trailer, ramp, tie‑down hardware, and lifting accessories. Record any deficiencies and correct them before proceeding.  
5. **Post‑operation check** – After transport, inspect the truck’s chassis, lifting lugs, and tie‑down points for signs of stress or damage. Repair any issues before the next use.
