## Maintenance Intervals – Excavator (Generic 20‑t Model)

The following section defines the routine service schedule, greasing points, filter‑change requirements, and under‑carriage (track/tyre) inspections for the generic 20‑t excavator. All time‑based intervals are taken directly from the **SPEC VALUES** table; no other numbers are introduced.

---

### PROCEDURE 001: SERVICE INTERVAL – 250 HOUR (Minor Service)

| Item | Every **h** | Work Items | Estimated Labor **h** |
|------|------------|------------|------------------------|
| service_250h | 250 | • Engine oil change  <br>• Engine oil filter <br>• Primary fuel filter <br>• Greasing of all listed points | 2.5 |

**Steps**

1. **Prepare the machine** – Park on level ground, engage the parking brake, and disconnect the battery.  
2. **Drain engine oil** – Remove the drain plug, collect oil in a suitable container, and dispose of it per local regulations.  
3. **Replace engine oil filter** – Unscrew the old filter, lubricate the new filter’s sealing ring with fresh oil, and install.  
4. **Replace primary fuel filter** – Follow the fuel‑system lock‑out procedure, remove the filter, install the new unit, and prime the system.  
5. **Refill engine oil** – Fill with the manufacturer‑specified grade to the correct level.  
6. **Greasing** – Apply the recommended grease to all greasing points listed in **Procedure 002**.  
7. **Final checks** – Verify oil level, check for leaks, and clear any fault codes.

> **WARNING:** Never operate the excavator until the engine oil has been refilled to the correct level. Low oil can cause catastrophic engine damage.

### PROCEDURE 002: GREASING POINTS (Every 250 h)

| Grease Point | Component | Frequency |
|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| Pivot pins on boom & arm | Boom & Arm | Every 250 h (service_250h) |
| Swing bearing pins | Swing drive | Every 250 h (service_250h) |
| Track rollers & idlers | Undercarriage | Every 250 h (service_250h) |
| Hydraulic cylinder pins | Hydraulic pump & cylinders | Every 250 h (service_250h) |

**Steps**

1. Clean each greasing point with a lint‑free rag to remove debris.  
2. Attach the grease gun and pump the recommended amount of high‑temperature grease.  
3. Wipe excess grease and re‑inspect for proper coverage.  

> **WARNING:** Over‑greasing can cause seal failure. Apply only the amount specified in the grease gun’s calibrated indicator.

### PROCEDURE 003: SERVICE INTERVAL – 500 HOUR (Intermediate Service)

| Item | Every **h** | Work Items | Estimated Labor **h** |
|------|------------|------------|------------------------|
| service_500h | 500 | • Hydraulic filter <br>• Outer air filter <br>• Secondary fuel filter <br>• Greasing (repeat Procedure 002) | 4.0 |

**Steps**

1. **Hydraulic filter** – Depressurize the hydraulic system, remove the old filter, install a new filter, and bleed the system.  
2. **Outer air filter** – Open the air‑filter housing, discard the used element, install a new outer element, and reseal.  
3. **Secondary fuel filter** – Follow the same lock‑out steps as the primary filter, replace, and prime.  
4. **Greasing** – Perform the greasing routine described in Procedure 002.  
5. **System checks** – Verify hydraulic pressure, listen for abnormal noises, and confirm no fault codes.

> **WARNING:** Do not restart the hydraulic system until the filter housing is securely closed and all pressure relief valves are re‑installed.

### PROCEDURE 04: SERVICE INTERVAL – 1 000 HOUR (Major Service)

| Item | Every **h** | Work Items | Estimated Labor **h** |
|------|------------|------------|------------------------|
| service_1000h | 1 000 | • Swing‑gear oil change <br>• Final‑drive oil change <br>• Coolant system test <br>• Greasing (repeat Procedure 002) | 6.0 |

**Steps**

1. **Swing‑gear oil** – Drain the swing‑gear oil reservoir, replace the oil filter if equipped, refill with the specified gear oil, and bleed air.  
2. **Final‑drive oil** – Remove the drain plug, collect old oil, replace the final‑drive filter (if applicable), refill to the correct level, and check for leaks.  
3. **Coolant test** – Perform a pressure test of the cooling system, inspect hoses and radiators, and replace coolant if the test fails.  
4. **Greasing** – Execute Procedure 002.  
5. **Functional test** – Operate the swing and travel functions at low speed, monitoring temperature and pressure gauges.

> **WARNING:** Using incorrect gear oil viscosity can lead to premature wear of the swing drive and final drive gears.

### PROCEDURE 05: SERVICE INTERVAL – 2 000 HOUR (Comprehensive Service)

| Item | Every **h** | Work Items | Estimated Labor **h** |
|------|------------|------------|------------------------|

**Steps**

1. **Hydraulic oil** – Drain the hydraulic reservoir, flush with clean water (if recommended), refill with the specified hydraulic oil, and purge air.  
2. **Coolant** – Fully drain the cooling system, flush with distilled water, refill with the manufacturer‑specified coolant mixture, and bleed.  
3. **Inner air filter** – Open the air‑filter housing, replace the inner element, and reseal.  
4. **Greasing** – Perform Procedure 002.  
5. **System verification** – Run the machine under load for a sufficient period, checking hydraulic pressure, coolant temperature, and oil levels.

> **WARNING:** Never mix different types of hydraulic oil; contamination can cause system failure.

### PROCEDURE 06: DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter) CLEAN – 4 500 HOUR

| Item | Every **h** | Work Items | Estimated Labor **h** |
|------|------------|------------|------------------------|
| dpf_clean | 4 500 | • DPF ash cleaning (service shop) | 5.0 |

**Steps**

1. **Schedule a qualified service facility** – DPF cleaning requires specialized equipment.  
2. **Remove the DPF** – Follow the engine‑removal procedure, disconnect all sensors and hoses.  
3. **Transport to cleaning shop** – Ensure the filter is sealed to prevent ash loss.  
4. **Re‑install DPF** – After cleaning, reinstall, reconnect sensors, and perform a diagnostic check for pressure drop.

> **WARNING:** Operating the excavator with a clogged DPF can cause excessive back‑pressure, leading to engine power loss and possible damage.

### PROCEDURE 07: UNDERCARRIAGE – TRACK / TYRE INSPECTION (Every 250 h)

| Component | Inspection Item | Acceptance Criteria |
|-----------|----------------|---------------------|
| Tracks | Wear depth, broken links, tension | Wear ≤ 3 mm; no broken links; tension within spec |
| Idlers & Rollers | Bearing condition, cracks | No excessive play; surface free of cracks |
| Tyres (if equipped) | Tread depth, sidewall damage, pressure | Tread ≥ 4 mm; no cuts; pressure per spec |

**Steps**

1. **Visual inspection** – Walk around the machine, looking for loose or missing track pins, cracked rollers, or tyre bulges.  
2. **Measure wear** – Use a calibrated gauge to check track wear depth; compare with the acceptance criteria.  
3. **Check tension** – Adjust track tension according to the manufacturer’s torque specifications.  
4. **Roller & idler bearing check** – Rotate each roller by hand; feel for roughness or play. Replace if abnormal.  
5. **Tyre pressure** – If tyres are fitted, check pressure with a calibrated gauge and adjust to the recommended value.  
6. **Record** – Log wear measurements, adjustments, and any parts replaced in the maintenance log.

> **WARNING:** Operating with improperly tensioned tracks can cause rapid wear, loss of traction, or catastrophic track failure.
